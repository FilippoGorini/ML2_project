# Evaluation practice and sliding-window conventions in sensor-based time-series classification (HAR, EEG, EMG)

Scope note: notes for defending (1) contiguous per-recording time-block splits with gaps plus leave-recordings-out, and (2) heavily overlapping windows (stride 10 / window 150) used only inside the training set. Sources accessed 2026-10-10. MDPI pages returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher, so MDPI papers were read via PMC or arXiv mirrors (URLs given).

## (a) Do random window-level splits / k-fold over windows inflate accuracy relative to subject-independent or temporally separated evaluation?

### Takeaway
Yes. This is the clear consensus in HAR. Every comparison found here reports that random or k-fold splits over windows score higher than leave-subject-out or group splits. The reported gaps run from about 5 to 36 points of accuracy or F1, and up to about 15 points for a single CNN (99.85% vs 85.1%). The mechanism is dependence between windows: shared samples when windows overlap, and temporal autocorrelation within a subject or session even when they do not. EEG work shows the extreme case: a block-structured confound alone can produce high accuracy that falls to chance once the confound is removed.

### Cited Findings
- **Hammerla & Plötz 2015 (UbiComp '15, pp. 1041–1051), "Let's (not) stick together: pairwise similarity biases cross-validation in activity recognition".** They show that standard k-fold CV is biased because samples are statistically dependent, "specially when using sliding windows" (their claim as quoted by Tello et al. 2023). They propose *meta-segmented cross-validation*, which keeps contiguous segments together in folds. Code: github.com/nhammerla/metaSegmentedXVAL. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/2750858.2807551); [code](https://github.com/nhammerla/metaSegmentedXVAL); [Tello et al. 2023 summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11950)
- **Dehghani, Glatard & Shihab 2019, "Subject Cross Validation in Human Activity Recognition", arXiv:1904.02666 [cs.LG].** I could verify this exact title only as an arXiv preprint and did not confirm a Sensors publication under it. The abstract says "samples produced by the same subjects are likely to be correlated". k-fold CV overestimates performance by about **10%**, and by about **16% when overlapping sliding windows are used**. Conclusion: HAR needs subject-wise CV. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02666)
- **Dehghani, Sarbishei, Glatard & Shihab 2019, Sensors 19(22):5026, "A Quantitative Comparison of Overlapping and Non-Overlapping Sliding Windows for HAR Using Inertial Sensors".** Datasets: (1) 17 subjects, 33 fitness activities, 9 IMUs; (2) 94 subjects, armband. Windows from 0.25 s to 7 s; overlapping windows use a 200 ms step. Under subject-dependent (k-fold) CV, overlap raised F1 by about **8–13% for k-NN and decision trees** and about 1% for NB and NCC. Under subject-independent CV the gain disappeared: on dataset 1, overlap *lowered* DT and k-NN F1 by 1–4%, and on dataset 2 it raised F1 by only 1–2%. They identify overlap as an extra dependency: "the overlap between adjacent windows is another source of dependency between data points." — [PMC6891351](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6891351/); [DOI 10.3390/s19225026](https://doi.org/10.3390/s19225026)
- **Gholamiangonabadi, Kiselov & Grolinger 2020, IEEE Access, "Deep Neural Networks for HAR With Wearable Sensors: Leave-One-Subject-Out Cross-Validation for Model Selection".** The same CNN scores **99.85% under 10-fold CV but 85.1% under LOSOCV**. The architecture that generalised best to new subjects under LOSO was a CNN with two convolutions, a 1D filter, a sliding window and vector magnitude. Their argument is that model selection should also use LOSO. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Deep-Neural-Networks-for-Human-Activity-Recognition-Gholamiangonabadi-Kiselov/272023fe1b116eca2c5836cc40d60abd412413c8); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343093625_Deep_Neural_Networks_for_Human_Activity_Recognition_With_Wearable_Sensors_Leave-One-Subject-Out_Cross-Validation_for_Model_Selection) (numbers taken from the abstract via search snippet; full text not read)
- **Tello, Degeler & Lazovik 2023, arXiv:2310.11950, "Too Good To Be True: performance overestimation in (re)current practices for HAR".** I did not verify a peer-reviewed venue. Their own experiments, random split vs group split:
  - MILAN with Random Forest: balanced accuracy 92.91% → 60.06%, weighted F1 95.17% → **56.64%**.
  - PAMAP2 with GNN: 90.90 → 85.50.
  - MHEALTH with GNN: 97.61 → 85.46.

  Summary sentence: "the performance drop with an unbiased evaluation goes from 5.4% to 33.6%". Their review of recent papers also found:
  - Wang et al. 2022 (IEEE Sensors J.): MotionSense F1 99.35 (random split) → **92.10** (subject-independent 5-fold).
  - Zimbelman & Keefe: best accuracy (about 89%) at 90% overlap with a random 67:33 split. Tello et al. conclude "the higher overlap, the higher overestimation".
  - Several papers report near-perfect scores on UCI-HAR, WISDM and PAMAP2 that are explained by shuffled train_test_split.

  The recommended fix is Group K-Fold, grouped by subject, collection date or another domain unit. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11950); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11950)
- **Lockhart & Weiss 2014 (UbiComp '14 Adjunct, HASCA workshop), "Limitations with Activity Recognition Methodology & Data Sets".** With Random Forest: impersonal/universal model (test users unseen) **76%**, personal model **98%**, hybrid model (test user's data also in training) **95%**. The hybrid score sits close to the personal one, so mixing test-subject data into training produces near-personal accuracy that an unseen-user deployment does not get. — [Fordham PDF](https://www.cis.fordham.edu/wisdm/Lockhart_Weiss_HASCA.pdf) (numbers via search summary)
- **Li, Johansen, Ahmed, Ilyevsky, Wilbur, Bharadwaj & Siskind 2021, IEEE TPAMI 43(1):316–333, "The Perils and Pitfalls of Block Design for EEG Classification Experiments".** Earlier work recorded all stimuli of one class in a contiguous block. Li et al. show that the high accuracies came from block-level temporal correlation, not from the stimulus. With a rapid-event design (classes intermixed), "classification accuracy drops to chance". They point to "the temporal autocorrelations that exist in all neuroimaging data", and note that cross-block classification also falls to chance. — [author PDF](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~qobi/papers/tpami2021.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/tpami.2020.2973153)
- **Hammerla, Halloran & Plötz 2016, IJCAI, "Deep, Convolutional, and Recurrent Models for HAR Using Wearables".** This is a well-cited deep HAR benchmark that splits by subject and run, never by window. Opportunity: validation = run 2 of subject 1, test = runs 4–5 of subjects 2 and 3. PAMAP2: validation = runs 1–2 of subject 5, test = runs 1–2 of subject 6. Daphnet: validation = run 1 of subject 9, test = runs 1–2 of subject 2. They also note that "Individual frames of movement data in ubicomp are usually treated as statistically independent". — [IJCAI PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/16/Papers/220.pdf)

### Inferences
- Splitting each recording into contiguous time blocks with a one-window gap is the within-recording analogue of the "group" splits these papers recommend: the group is a time block instead of a subject. It removes window-sharing leakage and most short-range autocorrelation leakage. It does not remove recording-level similarity, such as the same robot, terrain or session. The separate leave-recordings-out protocol is the direct analogue of LOSO and of Hammerla et al. 2016's held-out runs.
- Expect leave-recordings-out scores to be lower than block-split scores. The HAR literature treats that drop as the honest estimate of generalisation to new conditions, not as a model failure.

## (b) Common windowing practice: typical overlap, why overlap is used, and its measured effect

### Takeaway
Fixed-size sliding windows are the standard segmentation in HAR. 50% overlap is the most common choice, and higher overlaps (78%, 90%, over 95%) also appear. Overlap is used to get more training windows and to keep temporal context at window boundaries. Under subject-independent evaluation, though, Dehghani et al. 2019 found **no meaningful gain** from overlap, so most of the benefit reported in earlier papers came from leaky evaluation. That result argues against expecting large gains from overlap. It does not show that overlap inside the training set is harmful.

### Cited Findings
- **Banos, Galvez, Damas, Pomares & Rojas 2014, Sensors 14(4):6474–6499, "Window Size Impact in Human Activity Recognition".**
  - On prevalence: "The sliding window approach … is the most widely employed segmentation technique in activity recognition."
  - On overlap: "An overlap between adjacent windows is tolerated for certain applications; however, this is less frequently used."
  - They themselves used non-overlapping windows with 10-fold CV repeated 100 times.
  - Main findings: "the interval 1–2 s proves to provide the best trade-off between recognition speed and accuracy", with a cut-off around 1 s beyond which longer windows bring no significant benefit.
  - Caveat: their 10-fold CV pools windows, so it is itself the kind of evaluation criticised under (a).

  — [PMC4029702](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4029702/); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/14/4/6474)
- **Overlap levels seen in practice** (from Tello et al. 2023's survey of recent papers): 50% (UCI-HAR, with 128-sample windows at 50 Hz and a 64-sample step; WISDM-based works), 30/50/78% (Huang et al.), 12.5–50% (Wang et al. 2022), up to 90% (Zimbelman & Keefe), and over 95% in some works. One author justified overlap as being "to preserve temporal information". — [Tello et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11950)
- **Hammerla et al. 2016 (IJCAI)** used 1 s windows with 50% overlap (Opportunity, Daphnet) and 5.12 s windows with a 1 s step (78% overlap, PAMAP2, following Reiss & Stricker), with subject/run-wise held-out sets. — [IJCAI PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/16/Papers/220.pdf)
- **Dehghani et al. 2019 (Sensors 19:5026):** overlapping windows produce about **9×** more data and about **4×** more training time. Their conclusion: "The performance improvements regarding overlapping windowing reported in the literature seem to be associated with underlying limitations of subject-dependent cross validation". Under subject-independent CV, "non-overlapping sliding windows reach the same performance". **This argues against the expectation that overlap improves the model.** Note the scope: 200 ms steps, classical classifiers (DT, k-NN, NB, NCC) on hand-crafted features, no deep nets. — [PMC6891351](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6891351/); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-Quantitative-Comparison-of-Overlapping-and-for-Dehghani-Sarbishei/ebb22cc8848ae7d4d55eb02b2cb8ac073ef279db)

### Inferences
- For the robot project: a stride of 10 on a 150-sample window (93% overlap) is at the high end of HAR practice but within its range (78–95% appear). The honest justification is more, shift-augmented training examples and a dense label signal for a model that will run online at every timestep. It is not a claim that overlap itself improves accuracy.
- Dehghani's null result was for classical classifiers on features. Deep or gradient-boosted models trained on many windows may benefit more from shift-augmentation, but I found no paper here that shows this under leak-free evaluation (see Gaps). The student could test it directly by comparing a train stride of 150 with a train stride of 10 under the block split; the repo already has a stride-150 rerun.

## (c) Is train–test window overlap leakage? Is overlap within training fine? Do non-overlapping windows with a random split still leak?

### Takeaway
Window overlap *across* the train/test boundary is described as leakage: the test window has partly "already been seen". Several studies also show that **non-overlapping windows with a random split still inflate results**, because neighbouring windows from the same activity bout or recording are strongly correlated. So "non-overlapping + random split" is **not** an acceptable substitute for block or group splitting. Overlap *within* the training set is common in respected benchmarks that use held-out subjects or runs, and the criticism always targets the train/test boundary. I found no source that states outright that "overlap within the training set is fine"; that position is implicit, not quoted.

### Cited Findings
- **Overlap across the split = test data already seen.** Tello et al. 2023: "if w2 is randomly chosen for validation and w1 and w3 are kept for training, the classifier is tested on data that was already seen. Consequently, the performance … is overestimated." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11950)
- **Non-overlapping windows still leak through temporal correlation.** Tello et al. 2023 write that for non-overlapping windows "the dependence between consecutive windows is less evident. However, for long running activities (e.g., walking, standing, reading), the similarity between samples drawn in a short interval will create a strong correlation between consecutive windows". A randomly assigned test window "will be almost identical to those assigned to the training set". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11950)
- **Empirical: non-overlapping + random 10-fold vs LOSO.** Wang S. et al. 2018, Smart Health 9:179–191, "Eating detection and chews counting through sensing mastication muscle contraction", used non-overlapping fixed windows. Accuracy was **94.4% with random 10-fold**, 96.12% for per-user models, and **89% with LOSO**. Tello et al. comment: "even for non-overlapping windows, a random k-fold CV evaluation may produce overestimated results because of the temporal dependence between windows". — reported in [Tello et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11950) (primary not read)
- **Empirical: both overlapping and non-overlapping windows inflate under 10-fold vs LOSO on UCI-HAR.** Mekruksavanich & Jitpattanakul, five LSTM variants. Tello et al.: "this work evidences that the overestimation occurs using either overlapping and non-overlapping windows". The exact values are only in a figure (bars around 97–99% for 10-fold) and could not be extracted reliably. — [Tello et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11950)
- **Dehghani et al. arXiv 2019:** about 10% inflation from k-fold vs subject CV even without overlap, and about 16% with overlap. So overlap adds roughly 6 points on top of the autocorrelation and subject leak. — [arXiv:1904.02666](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02666)
- **Dehghani et al. Sensors 2019** name two sources of dependence: within-subject correlation ("samples drawn from a given subject are likely to not be independent") and overlap ("another source of dependency"). — [PMC6891351](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6891351/)
- **EEG analogue (Li et al. 2021, TPAMI):** with no window overlap at all, block-level temporal autocorrelation alone produced high accuracy that fell to chance under a design that broke the confound. This is the strongest evidence that temporal adjacency between train and test is a leak on its own. — [PDF](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~qobi/papers/tpami2021.pdf)
- **Overlap within training, separated test (implicit practice).** Hammerla et al. 2016 (IJCAI) trained on 50%- or 78%-overlap windows while validation and test were whole held-out runs of other subjects. This is the same structure as the student's design. — [IJCAI PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/16/Papers/220.pdf)

### Inferences
- The student's design matches the literature's recommendation. Contiguous blocks with gaps of at least one window length guarantee that no test window shares samples with a training window. The block structure, unlike random window assignment, also limits autocorrelation leakage to the block edges, which the gap absorbs. Dense overlap is confined to training, which is the setting no source criticises.
- **Non-overlapping windows + random split: not acceptable as the main protocol.** The Wang 2018 and Mekruksavanich results (via Tello 2023) and Dehghani's roughly 10% inflation without overlap show that inflation persists. The risk is likely *worse* at 1 kHz with 150-sample (0.15 s) windows: adjacent non-overlapping windows are 0.15 s apart within the same gait cycle and contact phase, so they are nearly identical. That is an inference, not a measured result.
- One-window gaps remove sample sharing but not longer-range autocorrelation, such as gait-cycle periodicity or slow drift. A conservative write-up could note that leave-recordings-out is the stronger check (the analogue of Li et al.'s cross-block test).
- Evaluating the test set at every timestep (stride 1) is fine as long as all test windows lie inside the test block. Overlap *among* test windows does not leak; it only makes test samples correlated, which affects confidence intervals (another researcher's topic), not bias.

### Points that cut against the choices (for balance)
- **Overlap may bring no real accuracy benefit** (Dehghani et al. 2019): it costs about 9× the data and 4× the training time for no gain under subject-independent CV. Defend stride 10 as augmentation or dense supervision, or show an ablation.
- **Within-subject evaluation is legitimate for personal models** (Lockhart & Weiss 2014: personal 98% vs impersonal 76%). If the deployment target is the *same* robot and conditions, the within-recording block split answers a legitimate question. It is still not random window-level shuffling, which nobody defends.
- **Banos et al. 2014 used non-overlapping windows with pooled 10-fold CV.** That shows such protocols were standard in highly cited work, but later papers (Dehghani 2019, Tello 2023) explicitly flag that kind of evaluation as optimistic.

### Gaps
- I found no explicit verbatim statement such as "overlap within the training set is acceptable; only train–test overlap is leakage". The support is the practice of benchmark papers like Hammerla 2016 and the way critics frame the problem.
- I could not extract exact numbers for Mekruksavanich & Jitpattanakul; I relied on Tello's figure description.
