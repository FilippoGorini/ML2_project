# Validating models on dependent (autocorrelated, time-ordered) data: random/k-fold vs blocked splits

Scope note: covers general statistics/ML literature only (no HAR- or legged-robot-specific papers, per assignment). Two sources touching sliding-window classification are included under Q6 because they are general-ML leakage papers; one of them uses HAR datasets as examples, flagged as such.

Sources actually read (full text or official page) for these notes: scikit-learn docs; Bergmeir, Hyndman & Koo 2018 (author PDF); Arlot & Celisse 2010 (arXiv full text); Kapoor & Narayanan 2023 (arXiv full text); Cerqueira, Torgo & Mozetič 2020 (arXiv full text); Zheng 2019 note on Racine 2000 (arXiv full text); Mozetič et al. 2018 (PLOS ONE page); Apicella et al. 2025 and Shi et al. 2026 (arXiv full text). Roberts et al. 2017, Racine 2000, Burman et al. 1994, Ploton et al. 2020 and López de Prado 2018 were NOT readable (paywall/403/404); claims about them below come from abstracts, author pages, or how other primary papers describe them, and are marked as such.

## Q1. Does the literature establish that random splits / standard k-fold are optimistically biased on dependent data? (incl. Roberts et al. 2017)

### Takeaway
Yes, this is consensus across statistics, ecology, ML-methodology and official tooling docs: when train and test observations are dependent, random splits put near-duplicates of test points into training, and the resulting error estimate is too optimistic (and CV-based model selection over-fits). The main documented exception is narrow (Q4).

### Cited Findings
- scikit-learn's official CV guide: "While i.i.d. data is a common assumption in machine learning theory, it rarely holds in practice. If one knows that the samples have been generated using a time-dependent process, it is safer to use a time-series aware cross-validation scheme. Similarly, if we know that the generative process has a group structure (samples collected from different subjects, experiments, measurement devices), it is safer to use group-wise cross-validation." — [scikit-learn User Guide, §3.1 Cross-validation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html)
- Same guide, time-series section: "classical cross-validation techniques such as KFold and ShuffleSplit assume the samples are independent and identically distributed, and would result in unreasonable correlation between training and testing instances (yielding poor estimates of generalization error) on time series data." — [scikit-learn User Guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html)
- Same guide, on shuffling: for time-ordered samples "shuffling the data will likely lead to a model that is overfit and an inflated validation score: it will be tested on samples that are artificially similar (close in time) to training samples." — [scikit-learn User Guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html)
- Arlot & Celisse (2010, Statistics Surveys 4:40–79), §8.3 "Time series and dependent observations": CV rests on the validation set being "independent from the training set with the same distribution. Therefore, when data ξ1,...,ξn are not independent, CV must be modified"; "when for instance data are positively correlated, Hart and Wehrly (1986) proved that CV overfits for choosing the bandwidth of a kernel estimator in regression." — [Arlot & Celisse 2010, arXiv:0907.4728](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4728)
- Roberts et al. (2017, Ecography 40(8):913–929): per the abstract summary, these structures "are regularly ignored, resulting in serious underestimation of predictive error"; they conclude that block cross-validation "is nearly universally more appropriate than random cross-validation if the goal is predicting to new data or predictor space, or for selecting causal predictors", and recommend block CV wherever dependence structures exist. (Abstract/author page only; full text not accessible here.) — [Hartig publication page](https://florianhartig.github.io/publications/roberts2017cross/); [DOI 10.1111/ecog.02881](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.02881); [ResearchGate entry](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/311523792_Cross-validation_strategies_for_data_with_temporal_spatial_hierarchical_or_phylogenetic_structure)
- Mozetič, Torgo, Cerqueira & Smailović (2018, PLOS ONE) — a time-ordered *classification* study (Twitter sentiment, large datasets): "standard cross-validation with random selection of examples is significantly worse than the blocked cross-validation, and should not be used to evaluate classifiers in time-ordered data scenarios"; "all cross-validation variants tend to overestimate the performance, while the sequential methods tend to underestimate it"; "no significant differences between the blocked cross-validation and the best sequential validation." — [Mozetič et al. 2018, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0194317)
- Cerqueira, Torgo & Mozetič (2020, Machine Learning 109), real-world case study of 62 time series: "In general, cross-validation procedures tend to under-estimate the error (i.e. are optimistic estimators), while the prequential and out-of-sample variants tend to overestimate the error (i.e. are pessimistic estimators)." — [Cerqueira et al., arXiv:1905.11744](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11744)
- Ploton et al. (2020, Nature Communications 11:4540), 11.8 million trees in central Africa, random forest biomass model: a standard non-spatial validation suggested the model explains more than half of biomass variation, while spatial validation accounting for spatial autocorrelation revealed "quasi-null" predictive power; ignoring spatial dependence "masks overfitting and leads to highly optimistic evaluations." (From abstract/search summary; full text redirected to a login.) — [Ploton et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-18321-y); [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v11y2020i1d10.1038_s41467-020-18321-y.html)
- Bergmeir, Hyndman & Koo (2018) themselves state the textbook problem: when K-fold removes random observations, "This removal invalidates the CV in the dependent setting because of the correlation between errors in the training and test sets." — [Bergmeir, Hyndman & Koo, author PDF](https://robjhyndman.com/papers/cv-wp.pdf)

### Inferences
- Magnitude of optimism is problem-dependent: from "slight" in well-specified stationary AR settings (Bergmeir et al.) to "more than half the variance vs ~zero" in the spatial case (Ploton). For 1 kHz proprioceptive data, adjacent samples are extremely correlated, which puts it closer to the severe end: a randomly shuffled window has near-identical neighbours in training.
- The distinction that matters (Roberts; Kapoor & Narayanan L3.2) is the *claim being made*: if the claim is "works on new/later data", random splits estimate the wrong quantity.

### Gaps
- Could not read Roberts et al. 2017 full text, so its specific simulation numbers (how much random CV underestimates error) are not quoted here.
- Exact Ploton R² values not verified from full text.

## Q2. Recommended alternatives: blocked CV, h-block, hv-block, gaps/buffers, forward chaining, purged/embargoed CV, leave-one-group-out — and how is the gap chosen?

### Takeaway
All alternatives share one idea: make training and evaluation data approximately independent by (i) keeping contiguous blocks and (ii) removing a buffer of h observations at the train/test boundary, with h set to the range beyond which observations are (nearly) independent; for non-stationary series or forecasting claims, preserve temporal order (hold-out / forward chaining). For grouped data (recordings, subjects, devices), hold out whole groups.

### Cited Findings
- **Modified CV / gap idea (Arlot & Celisse):** "The main approach used in the literature for solving this issue is to choose I(t) and I(v) such that min |i − j| > h > 0, where h controls the distance from which observations i and j are independent." For short-range dependence, observations are "almost independent" when |i−j| > h "is large enough"; optimality results exist for α-mixing data with h = h_n → ∞ "not too fast" (Hart & Vieu 1990). — [Arlot & Celisse 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4728)
- **h-block CV (Burman, Chow & Nolan 1994, Biometrika 81(2):351–358):** leave-one-out where the h observations before and after the test point are also removed from training, plus a correction term; Arlot & Celisse: "The h-block CV (Burman et al., 1994) is modified CV plus a corrective term ... Simulation experiments in several (short range) dependent frameworks show that this corrective term matters when h/n is not small." Bergmeir et al. describe it as "the h observations preceding and following the observation are left out" and call it "non-dependent cross-validation". (Original paper not accessed; description via these two primary papers.) — [Arlot & Celisse 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4728); [Bergmeir, Hyndman & Koo 2018](https://robjhyndman.com/papers/cv-wp.pdf)
- **hv-block CV (Racine 2000, J. Econometrics 99(1):39–61):** test set is a contiguous block of 2v+1 observations, and "another h samples (as the gaps)" are removed on both sides before training. "The parameter h controls the level of dependence between the training set and the test set. It can either be small such that h/n → 0 ... or it can be large such that h/n ≡ p for some p ∈ (0, ½) as required by Burman et al. (1994)." With v = 0 it reduces to h-block, which Racine showed is not consistent in Shao's (1993) sense (tends to choose overly large models); Racine requires v large such that n_v/n_c → ∞. — [Zheng 2019, arXiv:1910.08904](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08904); [EconPapers record of Racine 2000](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:econom:v:99:y:2000:i:1:p:39-61)
- **Debate on hv-block theory:** Zheng (2019) shows hv-block "is not a balanced incomplete block design (BIBD)", so Racine's consistency argument via Shao (1993) does not go through and "the theoretical consistency of hv-block remains" open; empirically Racine found hv-block better than h-block and gapless CV. — [Zheng 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08904)
- **How h is chosen in practice:** the theory gives only asymptotic rates (above); the practical rule across sources is "large enough that observations further apart are effectively independent", i.e. tied to the autocorrelation range (Arlot & Celisse). Bergmeir et al.'s simulation implementation removes training rows "if they have a lag distance smaller than p from a row in the test set, where p is the maximal model order" — i.e. h = the input-window (embedding) length. — [Bergmeir, Hyndman & Koo 2018](https://robjhyndman.com/papers/cv-wp.pdf)
- **Blocked CV and hv-blocked CV as described by Cerqueira et al.:** blocked CV = k-fold with contiguous folds and "no initial randomly shuffle of observations"; hv-blocked "also removes adjacent observations between the training and test sets. Effectively, this creates a gap between both sets." Prequential "in blocks with a gap block" exists too; "The rationale behind this idea is to increase the independence between training and test sets." — [Cerqueira et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11744)
- **Hold-out / forward evaluation for non-stationary series (Arlot & Celisse):** "using CV for non-stationary time-series is a quite difficult problem. The only reasonable approach in general is the hold-out, that is, I(t) = {1,...,m} and I(v) = {m+1,...,n} for some deterministic m." — [Arlot & Celisse 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4728)
- **TimeSeriesSplit (scikit-learn):** "Provides train/test indices to split time-ordered data, where other cross-validation methods are inappropriate, as they would lead to training on future data and evaluating on past data." Successive training sets "are supersets of those that come before them." `gap`: "Number of samples to exclude from the end of each train set before the test set" (added in v0.24). — [sklearn TimeSeriesSplit API](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.TimeSeriesSplit.html); [User Guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html)
- **Group-wise CV (GroupKFold, LeaveOneGroupOut):** "The i.i.d. assumption is broken if the underlying generative process yields groups of dependent samples ... we need to ensure that all the samples in the validation fold come from groups that are not represented at all in the paired training fold." LeaveOneGroupOut "holds out samples belonging to one specific group." — [scikit-learn User Guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html)
- **Purged and embargoed CV (López de Prado 2018, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, Wiley, ch. 7):** addresses leakage from *overlapping labels*: a label at time t is computed from a window spanning several bars, so adjacent samples share information. Purging removes from training any observation whose label/information window overlaps any test observation's window; an embargo additionally drops training observations immediately after each test block to absorb residual serial correlation. (Book not accessed; described from secondary sources.) — [Wikipedia: Purged cross-validation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation); [quantmemo explainer](https://quantmemo.com/concepts/purged-embargoed-cv)
- **Kapoor & Narayanan:** block CV can "partition the dataset strategically so that the performance evaluation does not suffer from data leakage and overoptimism (Roberts et al., 2017; Valavi et al., 2021)." — [Kapoor & Narayanan, arXiv:2207.07048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048)

### Inferences
- The project's design [train 70% | gap | val 15% | gap | test 15%] per recording, gap = one window length, is a single-origin hold-out with an hv-block-style buffer, applied within each of 15 groups. It matches: Arlot & Celisse's hold-out for non-stationary series; sklearn's TimeSeriesSplit `gap` concept (one split); purging (removing windows that share samples across the boundary); and Bergmeir et al.'s h = embedding length. The "protocol B" rerun (unseen recordings) corresponds to LeaveOneGroupOut / group-wise CV.
- Gap = window length is the *minimum* that removes shared raw samples (purging). Whether it also removes dependence of the underlying process depends on the autocorrelation range of signals/labels (embargo idea). With 15% blocks of long recordings, the gap is tiny relative to block size, so even if residual dependence exists it affects only the edges of the val/test block.

### Gaps
- No source found giving a non-asymptotic, practical formula for h beyond "autocorrelation range" / "model order". Roberts et al. reportedly discuss block size vs. autocorrelation range, but the text could not be verified.
- Did not access Hyndman & Athanasopoulos FPP (tsCV / rolling origin) or Tashman 2000 directly; Valavi et al. blockCV and Schnaubelt 2019 not fetched.

## Q3. Bergmeir & Benítez 2012 and Bergmeir, Hyndman & Koo 2018 — exact conclusions and conditions (the main "against blocking" evidence)

### Takeaway
Debated/nuanced, but narrow: standard K-fold is shown valid only for *purely autoregressive* models (inputs = lagged values of the target) on *stationary* series *when model residuals are serially uncorrelated*. Underfit/misspecified models break it, in the optimistic direction. Later real-world evidence (Cerqueira et al. 2020) reverses the preference when series are non-stationary.

### Cited Findings
- BHK 2018 abstract: "in the case of a purely autoregressive model, the use of standard K-fold CV is possible as long as the models considered have uncorrelated errors. Such a setup occurs, for example, when the models nest a more appropriate model." K-fold CV "performs favourably compared to both OOS evaluation and other time-series-specific techniques such as non-dependent cross-validation." Published in Computational Statistics & Data Analysis 120:70–83. — [Bergmeir, Hyndman & Koo, author PDF](https://robjhyndman.com/papers/cv-wp.pdf); [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/csdana/v120y2018icp70-83.html)
- Conditions/caveats (BHK 2018): "In the case of correlated errors, the CV procedure will be biased"; "only if the models underfit the data and lead to heavily correlated errors, are the cross-validation procedures to be avoided as in such a case they may yield a systematic underestimation of the error. However, this case can be easily detected by checking the residuals for serial correlation, e.g., using the Ljung-Box test." — [BHK 2018](https://robjhyndman.com/papers/cv-wp.pdf)
- Their simulations: series of 200 points, AR(3)/MA(1)/misspecified DGPs, one-step-ahead AR and MLP models. Non-dependent (h-block-style) CV "performs considerably worse, which is due to the fact that the fitted models are less accurate as they are fitted with less data." In the heavily misspecified experiment "the advantage of the CV procedures ... has nearly vanished, and the CV estimates are more biased than the estimates obtained with the OOS procedure." — [BHK 2018](https://robjhyndman.com/papers/cv-wp.pdf)
- Bergmeir & Benítez (2012, Information Sciences, "On the use of cross-validation for time series predictor evaluation"): as summarised by Cerqueira et al., on stationary series CV gave more accurate estimates than OOS, "Despite the theoretical issue of applying standard cross-validation, they found no practical problem in their experiments. Notwithstanding, the Blocked cross-validation is suggested for performance estimation using stationary time series." (Original not accessed.) — [Cerqueira et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11744)
- Cerqueira et al. 2020 counter-evidence: synthetic stationary results reproduce Bergmeir's, "However ... the results from the synthetic stationary case studies do not reflect those obtained using real-world time series"; "when non-stationarities are present, [CV variants] systematically provide worse estimations than the out-of-sample approaches"; recommend order-preserving methods, specifically repeated hold-out over multiple test periods. They also note that for "large scale data sets the recommendation by Dietterich ... is to apply a simple out-of-sample estimation procedure (Holdout)." — [Cerqueira et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11744)

### Inferences
- The BHK result does not cover the project's setting: the task is classification of a *different* variable (contact) from multichannel exogenous sensor windows, not pure autoregression of the target; contact labels are piecewise-constant and therefore strongly serially correlated, so classifier errors are almost certainly serially correlated (errors cluster around transitions), which is exactly BHK's failure condition. Within-recording non-stationarity (gait changes, terrain, drift) also points to Cerqueira's real-world regime.
- The BHK paper's motivation (n = 200, data efficiency) is weak for a 1 kHz dataset with millions of samples, where Dietterich's hold-out recommendation (via Cerqueira) applies.

### Gaps
- Bergmeir & Benítez 2012 original text not read; exact volume/pages not verified here.

## Q4. Do non-overlapping windows plus a random split remove the problem?

### Takeaway
No source endorses this. Non-overlap removes *shared raw samples* (mechanical overlap) but not the autocorrelation of the underlying process; the evidence that random splits are optimistic comes largely from data with no overlap at all (tweets, forest plots), so dependence alone is enough.

### Cited Findings
- Dependence, not overlap, is the root issue in the theory: observations must be separated by more than h, where h is "the distance from which observations i and j are independent." — [Arlot & Celisse 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4728)
- Random CV was significantly worse than blocked CV on time-ordered tweets, which are discrete non-overlapping items. — [Mozetič et al. 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0194317)
- Random CV was strongly optimistic for spatially autocorrelated but distinct forest plots. — [Ploton et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-18321-y)
- López de Prado's embargo exists precisely because serial correlation outlives the label overlap: purging alone (removing overlap) is not deemed sufficient. — [Wikipedia: Purged cross-validation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation)
- Shi, Zhang & Shi (2026, arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed; uses HAR datasets as examples, but the analysis is generic to sliding-window TSC): mechanical overlap gives a minimum correlation range K0 = L/S − 1 windows (L = window length, S = stride), so at S = L overlap-induced correlation is zero, yet they still model "extended AR(1) dependence" on top; confidence-interval inflation from dependence "is not universal at zero overlap." — [Shi et al., arXiv:2609.30721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30721)

### Inferences
- For the project: the stride-150 (non-overlapping) rerun removes shared samples, but neighbouring 150 ms windows of a 1 kHz robot gait still share gait phase, contact state and slowly varying signals, so a random split of those windows would still put the test window's immediate neighbours in training. The block split is the right control; the stride-150 rerun is evidence for a different question (whether results depend on overlapping-window count).
- An empirical check that would make the argument data-driven: autocorrelation of features/labels vs lag, or compare random-split vs block-split scores on the stride-150 data (expect random > block).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed general-ML paper found that quantifies the residual optimism of random splits on *non-overlapping* windows of a continuous signal.

## Q5. Kapoor & Narayanan 2023 taxonomy entries relevant to temporal leakage / non-independence

### Takeaway
Two entries apply directly: [L3.1] temporal leakage and [L3.2] non-independence between train and test; their model info sheet asks authors to justify both.

### Cited Findings
- Published as Kapoor & Narayanan, "Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science", Patterns (2023); leakage found affecting 329 papers across 17 fields, 8-type taxonomy. — [arXiv:2207.07048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048)
- "[L3.1] Temporal leakage. When an ML model is used to make predictions about a future outcome of interest, the test set should not contain any data from a date before the training set." — [Kapoor & Narayanan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048)
- "[L3.2] Nonindependence between train and test samples. Nonindependence between train and test samples constitutes leakage, unless the scientific claim is about a distribution that has the same dependence structure." "The train-test split should account for the dependencies in the data"; block CV avoids "data leakage and overoptimism"; and "Handling nonindependence ... in general ... is a hard problem, since we might not know the underlying dependency structure." — [Kapoor & Narayanan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048)
- Model info sheet: for L3.2 "Researchers need to reason about the dependencies that may exist in their dataset and outline how dependencies across training and test sets are addressed." — [Kapoor & Narayanan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048)
- Nuance: in their civil-war case study they report "no substantial difference" between k-fold and an out-of-sample split once other leakage was fixed — i.e. temporal k-fold leakage was not the dominant error there. — [Kapoor & Narayanan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048)
- Apicella, Isgrò & Prevete (2025, Artificial Intelligence Review) extend this: sensor time series "are usually segmented into overlapping frames ... consecutive frames are not independent of each other, violating the ... (i.i.d.) condition needed in several validation frameworks (such as cross validation). Furthermore, in cases of overlapping frames, identical parts of the signal can be distributed into more than one frame." — [Apicella et al., arXiv:2401.13796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13796); [published version](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-025-11326-3)

### Inferences
- L3.2's escape clause ("unless the claim is about a distribution with the same dependence structure") is the honest framing for the project: a within-recording block split supports a claim about later segments of seen recordings; protocol B supports the stronger claim about unseen recordings.

### Gaps
- None material.

## Q6. Does any source address sliding windows sharing timesteps and recommend gaps of at least one window length? Plus: where blocking/gaps are pessimistic or debated

### Takeaway
The "gap ≥ one window length" rule is implied by purging (remove any training sample whose information window overlaps a test sample's) and by Bergmeir et al.'s non-dependent CV (h = lag order); no source found states "one window length" as a named rule for classification windows. Blocking and gaps have a documented cost: slight pessimism and wasted data.

### Cited Findings
- Bergmeir et al.'s non-dependent CV removes training rows within "a lag distance smaller than p" (the input window length) of any test row — equivalent to a gap of one input window. — [BHK 2018](https://robjhyndman.com/papers/cv-wp.pdf)
- Purging removes training observations whose label/information window overlaps the test window; embargo adds a further buffer. — [Wikipedia: Purged cross-validation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation)
- Overlapping frames put "identical parts of the signal" into more than one frame. — [Apicella et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13796)
- Overlap gives a mechanical correlation range of L/S − 1 windows. — [Shi et al. 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30721)
- **Against/costs of blocking and gaps:**
  - Gaps reduce training data, making fitted models worse and estimates pessimistic: nonDepCV "performs considerably worse ... as they are fitted with less data." — [BHK 2018](https://robjhyndman.com/papers/cv-wp.pdf)
  - Sequential / out-of-sample methods "tend to underestimate" performance. — [Mozetič et al. 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0194317)
  - Prequential and out-of-sample variants "tend to overestimate the error (i.e. are pessimistic estimators)." — [Cerqueira et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11744)
  - A single hold-out origin is high-variance: "By using a single origin, one is prone to" idiosyncrasies of that period; Cerqueira et al. (following Tashman) recommend repeated hold-out over multiple test periods. — [Cerqueira et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11744)
  - h-block (v = 0) is inconsistent for model selection (favours larger models), and hv-block's consistency is unproven. — [Zheng 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08904)
  - Roberts et al. are reported to note that blocking can force extrapolation into new predictor space, which can make blocked estimates pessimistic if the deployment target is interpolation. (Not verified from full text.) — [DOI 10.1111/ecog.02881](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.02881)

### Inferences
- Consensus: random splits are optimistic on dependent data; some form of blocking/grouping is required when the claim is about new data. Debated: whether plain K-fold is acceptable (only for well-specified pure AR on stationary series), the exact gap size, and blocked CV vs hold-out (stationary → blocked CV slightly better; non-stationary → order-preserving hold-out better).
- For this project the known costs are small: the gap is one window (≈0.1% of data); per-recording blocks with 15 recordings average out some single-origin variance; the expected bias direction is slight pessimism, which is the safe side for a reported test score.
- A weakness worth stating honestly: hyperparameters are tuned on one fixed validation block per recording (single origin, no repeats), so selection could fit that block's idiosyncrasies; the literature's remedy is repeated hold-out or blocked CV with gaps on the training part.

### Gaps
- No primary source found that explicitly states "gap ≥ one window length" for overlapping sliding-window *classification*; the rule is an inference from purging and from h = embedding order.
- López de Prado's book text and his suggested embargo size were not verified from the primary source.
